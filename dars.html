DARS Redesign

Turning long, wordy degree audits into a clear plan for what’s next.

Timeline
Summer 2026
Role
UI/UX Designer
Tools

Overview

UCLA’s degree audit buries what students need under pages of text.

UCLA’s Degree Audit Report System (DARS) presents students’ degree progress as one long, cluttered page. Fulfilled and unfulfilled requirements are listed sequentially, interrupted by long, unformatted lists of courses that could satisfy each gap. This density leaves little visual hierarchy for students to navigate, making the interface intimidating for first-time users. This project set out to identify the root causes of DARS’s usability issues and redesign the interface so key information is clear and impossible to overlook.

Previous experience

  1. One long, cluttered pageFulfilled and unfulfilled requirements are listed sequentially with little visual hierarchy.
  2. Course lists in the wayLong, unformatted lists of eligible courses interrupt each requirement.
  3. No connection to planningStudents flip back and forth between their audit and the class planner.

Solution

From one long list to a dashboard students can read at a glance.

I redesigned DARS around what students actually look for first, and made the next step, finding a class that fills a gap, one click away.

Redesigned experience

  1. A bento dashboardGPA, completed units, and incomplete requirements are visible at a glance.
  2. Class search by requirementFilter unfulfilled requirements to find eligible classes and add them to the planner.
  3. An AI assistantQuick, clarifying questions answered in a familiar chat interface.

Research

Students avoid DARS until they have no choice.

To understand the scope of this problem, we conducted user interviews with UCLA students to learn how they actually navigate DARS and where it breaks down for them. These conversations confirmed that the consequences go beyond frustration: some students have missed critical information buried in the audit, leading them to enroll in classes they didn’t actually need. Here are some of the key insights we found:

Unpleasant to use

Participants described the current interface as annoying, confusing, and overwhelming.

Poorly displays graduation progress

Despite all of the necessary information being displayed, users still feel unconfident and make mistakes planning their next steps.

New students avoid using DARS

Students reported that they often avoided using DARS in their 1st and 2nd years, only using it in their later years out of necessity.

No integration with other tools

No integration with other highly used tools, like UCLA’s class planner, forces students to repeatedly look back-and-forth when planning classes.

How might we help students see where they stand and plan their next courses without scanning through pages of requirements?

Iterations

Testing showed we were close, but not there yet.

I designed the dashboard and AI chatbot. A groupmate created the first iteration of class search, which I redesigned for the final version. We tested our first iterations with 10 UCLA students.

Final designs

A dashboard that makes graduation progress obvious.

Reflection

Lived experience made the research sharper

I’ve felt the same frustrations with DARS as our users, which made this project especially fulfilling. It was my first chance to apply UX research methods hands-on and practice designing, testing and iterating.

What I would do differently

Our participants all came from our UX class. Next time, I’d test with incoming freshmen who’ve never used DARS, since many students told us DARS intimidated them when they were freshmen.

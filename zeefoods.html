Overview

Replacing phone orders with a self-serve platform.

TLDR

As the sole UI/UX designer during an 8-week internship at ZeeFoods, a wholesale Indian food distributor in the SF Bay Area, I designed a 0-to-1 order tracking and placement platform to replace their entirely phone-based ordering process. Through interviews with the owner and my project manager, I learned the customer base skewed older and less tech-savvy, which shaped a deliberately simple, low-learning-curve interface. The result was a self-serve system spanning login, catalog browsing, order tracking, and internal fulfillment management via a kanban board.

Problem space

ZeeFoods is a wholesale Indian food distributor serving the San Francisco Bay Area. Before this project, order placement and tracking ran entirely through phone calls.

Besides being plainly outdated, this method left orders prone to miscommunication, made it difficult for customers to check on order progress without calling again, and gave ZeeFoods no scalable way to manage growing order volume.

My role was to design a 0-to-1 web application that could replace this phone-based workflow with a self-serve ordering and tracking system for both ZeeFoods and its customers.

Research

Designing for customers who prefer the phone.

In order to better understand ZeeFoods’s customer-base, I developed a user persona and performed user research. I ran a short survey with 10 of ZeeFoods’s actual customers to get a clearer read on their comfort with technology and current pain points, with the results below:

xx%reported feeling “somewhat” or “not at all” comfortable navigating typical B2B ordering software
xx%said they’d abandoned or struggled with a digital order system before due to confusing navigation
xx%of participants were ages 40 through 60 years old; a demographic commonly uncomfortable approaching technology

This research shaped nearly every design decision going forward, and communicated to me that the interface needed to prioritize clarity and simplicity over novelty.

Needs

  • A simple way to place recurring orders without relearning a new system each time
  • Clear visibility into order status without having to call and ask

Frustrations

  • Orders placed by phone could be miscommunicated or forgotten
  • No way to check order status independently

Iterations

Grey over blue, kanban over bars.

Early on, I explored many different designs that I would frequently go over with my project manager and developers with. Wireframing became an essential skill to easily communicate my designs with other teams and stakeholders.

Blue dashboard or grey dashboard?

I tested a more colorful, blue-toned dashboard concept. But after weighing it against our target users’ comfort level with technology, I moved toward a cleaner grey dashboard, reserving color specifically for status indicators so users could scan order states at a glance without the interface feeling cluttered or overwhelming.

Bar or kanban view?

Another design decision I considered was either using a bar or kanban board view for the orders page that ZeeFoods’s warehouse workers would use to check on order statuses. Eventually, my project manager and I agreed to use the kanban board view because of its compactness and accessibility. Workers could easily see all orders on one page, as opposed to the bar view, which would require 3 separate pages.

Solution

Simple enough for a first-time user.

The final solution brings together both the needs and goals of ZeeFoods’s user base while also considering their age demographic and familiarities with technologies.
